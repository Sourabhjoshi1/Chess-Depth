Your improvement roadmap

Find your level and focus on what matters most right now. Ratings are rough guides (online rapid or FIDE).

Stage 1 · New to chess

0 – 800

  • How the pieces move, special rules
  • Basic mates: queen and rook vs king
  • Don't leave pieces undefended
  • Opening principles

Focus: safety of your pieces

Stage 2 · Beginner

800 – 1200

  • Forks, pins, skewers, discoveries
  • The CCT habit before every move
  • King and pawn endgames, opposition
  • One simple opening per side

Focus: daily tactics

Stage 3 · Improver

1200 – 1600

  • Combinations of 2–3 moves
  • Piece activity and weak squares
  • Lucena and Philidor positions
  • Analysing your own games

Focus: calculation and plans

Stage 4 · Club player

1600 – 2000

  • Pawn structures and typical plans
  • A deeper opening repertoire
  • Practical rook endgames
  • Time management, tournament play

Focus: strategy and endgames

Stage 5 · Advanced

2000+

  • Deep calculation training
  • Opponent-specific preparation
  • Complex endgames, master games
  • Psychology and consistency

Focus: depth and consistency

Opening guide

Know your openings

Popular openings, what they aim for and who they suit. Learn the ideas first; the moves follow. Read more in Building a Solid Opening Repertoire.

Italian Game

1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bc4

Open & tacticalBeginner+

Quick development and pressure on f7. Later White prepares c3 and d4 to build a big centre.

Explore on Lichess

Ruy Lopez (Spanish Game)

1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bb5

StrategicIntermediate+

Long-term pressure on Black's e5 pawn and centre. Rich middlegames with slow manoeuvring.

Explore on Lichess

Queen's Gambit

1.d4 d5 2.c4

Solid & strategicIntermediate+

White offers a wing pawn to remove Black's centre pawn and gain control of the centre.

Explore on Lichess

London System

1.d4 d5 2.Bf4

Solid systemBeginner+

The same easy setup (d4, Bf4, e3, Nf3, c3) against almost anything. Less theory to learn.

Explore on Lichess

English Opening

1.c4

FlexibleAdvanced

Control d5 from the side and keep many options open. Often transposes to other openings.

Explore on Lichess

Open Game (1...e5)

1.e4 e5

ClassicalBeginner+

Meet White in the centre and develop naturally. The best way to learn open positions.

Explore on Lichess

Sicilian Defence

1.e4 c5

Sharp & combativeIntermediate+

Unbalance the game. Black fights for d4 from the side and often counter-attacks on the queenside.

Explore on Lichess

Caro-Kann Defence

1.e4 c6

SolidBeginner+

Prepare ...d5 with a strong pawn chain, and develop the light-squared bishop outside it.

Explore on Lichess

French Defence

1.e4 e6

Solid & counter-attackingIntermediate+

Challenge the centre with ...d5, then attack White's pawn chain with ...c5 and ...f6.

Explore on Lichess

Queen's Gambit Declined

1.d4 d5 2.c4 e6

Classical & solidBeginner+

Hold the d5 point firmly, develop calmly, and free the position later with ...c5 or ...e5.

Explore on Lichess

Slav Defence

1.d4 d5 2.c4 c6

SolidIntermediate+

Defend d5 with the c-pawn, keeping the light-squared bishop free to develop.

Explore on Lichess

King's Indian Defence

1.d4 Nf6 2.c4 g6

Dynamic & attackingAdvanced

Let White take the centre, then strike back with ...e5 or ...c5 and often attack on the kingside.

Explore on Lichess

Nimzo-Indian Defence

1.d4 Nf6 2.c4 e6 3.Nc3 Bb4

StrategicAdvanced

Pin the c3 knight to control e4, and be ready to give the bishop pair for a better pawn structure.

Tactics glossary

The tactical patterns to know

Almost every combination is built from these patterns. Learn to name them, and you will start to see them. Try them out in Mastering Basic Chess Tactics.

Fork

One piece attacks two or more enemy pieces at once.

Pin

A piece can't move without exposing a more valuable piece behind it.

Skewer

A valuable piece is attacked and, when it moves, the piece behind it is lost.

Discovered attack

Moving one piece uncovers an attack by the piece behind it.

Double check

Two pieces give check at once, so the king must move.

Removing the defender

Capture or chase away the piece that protects a target.

Deflection

Force a defender to leave the square or piece it is guarding.

Decoy

Lure a piece (often the king) onto a square where it can be attacked.

Overloading

One piece has two defensive jobs and can't do both.

Interference

Block the line between a defender and the piece it protects.

Zwischenzug

An "in-between move": a stronger move played before the expected recapture.

X-ray

A piece attacks or defends a square through another piece.

Back-rank mate

A rook or queen mates a king trapped behind its own pawns.

Smothered mate

A knight mates a king that is surrounded by its own pieces.

Desperado

A piece that will be lost anyway grabs as much as it can first.

FAQ

Questions about these resources

Is everything on this page free?

Yes. The analyser, board editor, guides and lists are free for everyone. Books are sold separately by their publishers.

Why doesn't the game analyser find my account?

It works with public Lichess accounts only (Chess.com isn't supported yet). Check the spelling of the username and make sure the account has played rated games.

Where should a complete beginner start?

Follow Stage 1 of the roadmap: learn the basic mates, use the Lichess Practice lessons, and solve a few easy puzzles every day. A coach makes this much faster.

Which opening should I learn first?

Start with open games: the Italian Game as White and 1...e5 as Black. They teach development and centre control better than anything else.

How much should I study to improve?

Even 3 hours a week works if it's regular and balanced. See the training plan above, and remember to analyse your own games.

Should I use an engine to study?

Use it to check your analysis, not to replace it. Think first, write down your ideas, then compare with the engine. See How to Analyse Your Own Games.

How do I get the ChessDepth worksheets?

Students can download them from the Student Area. Not a student yet? Book a free demo class.
