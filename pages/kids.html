Online chess classes for kids

Live lessons with FIDE-rated coaches, in small groups or one-to-one. Your child learns to concentrate, plan ahead and handle wins and losses, and has fun doing it.

What your child gets

A real course, not just games online

Every child follows a step-by-step path from first moves to tournament play. Each class builds on the last, and you can see the progress.

Live classes with a coach

8–10 classes a month, 60–70 minutes each, in a small group or one-to-one. Children ask questions and solve puzzles during the lesson.

A clear level-by-level path

Beginner, intermediate and advanced stages. Your child is placed by level after the demo and moves up when ready.

Worksheets and homework

Printable worksheets after classes keep the ideas fresh between lessons. Short, regular practice works best for children.

A quiz every 8 sessions

Quizzes show what your child has learned. The coach shares feedback on strengths and the next things to work on.

Friendly online tournaments

Children play other students in our club tournaments on Lichess and Chess.com, a safe first step towards rated events.

Free practice tools

A daily puzzle, a blindfold trainer that starts with square colours, and free worksheet packs.

How classes change with age

Taught the way your child learns

Ages 5–7

Play first

Short, playful lessons and mini-games: how each piece moves, capturing safely, and the first checkmates. Lots of praise and small wins keep them keen.

Ages 8–12

Think before you move

Tactics such as forks, pins and skewers, opening principles and simple endgames. Children learn a checking routine, so they blunder less.

Ages 13–17

Play to compete

Openings, planning, endgame technique and game analysis. Students who want to play rated events get tournament preparation too.

Why chess

Skills that last beyond the board

Chess asks a child to sit still, look carefully, compare options and live with the result. Those habits carry over to homework, exams and everyday choices.

Read our parent's guide to chess for kids

  • ConcentrationA game needs focus for long stretches.
  • Planning aheadGood moves come from thinking two or three steps on.
  • Decision-makingLook at the options, compare, then choose.
  • ResilienceEvery player loses. Children learn to try again.
  • SportsmanshipShake hands, respect the opponent, follow the rules.
